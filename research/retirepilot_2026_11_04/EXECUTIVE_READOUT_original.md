## Three examples from the synthetic consumer roleplays

1. **Housing downsizing (S020):** Do not say that the property sale price is liquid retirement cash. Calculate sale settlement and officially required CPF refund; allocate potential CPF recredit separately and test the replacement housing timeline. If the sale cash would not fund the new home, report the financing gap rather than invent a bridge loan.
2. **CPF payout deferral (S030):** Compare two member-transcribed CPF estimates and the interim accessible-cash bridge. Never use an official published example as the actual member's payout or assume a universal percentage increase.
3. **Divorce (S012):** Do not infer asset ownership, property division or spouse CPF. Show individual, documented facts and identify missing settlement terms. Any scenario requiring invented allocations receives protective refusal.

## Nine policy defaults for the product owner to approve or revise

- D08: Future CPF statutory rules after the published years are **planning assumptions**, never verified future law.
- D09: Cash gaps use available liquid cash above a defined reserve, then owner-approved liquid assets; no forced borrowing or restricted CPF withdrawals.
- D10: Show age 90, 95 and 100 horizons; do not present a single predicted lifetime.
- D11: Do not declare downsizing affordable without a documented replacement financing path.
- D12: Unverified insurance, subsidies and inheritances excluded from the baseline; optionally stress as conditional cases.
- D13: Overseas retirement only supported through dated cash and FX facts until jurisdiction specifics are sourced.
- D14: No persona confidence percentage; show cash runway, shortfall and assumptions instead.
- D15: Where household data is unavailable or permission is withheld, show only confirmed personal assets and label missing household data unknown.
- D16: Multiple scenarios compose via dated, owner-specific transactions with conflict detection and no double counting.
